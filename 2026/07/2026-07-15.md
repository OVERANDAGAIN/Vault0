We agree with the reviewer that the divergence between the NLI-based and LLM-based metrics is surprising. We claim that this is a reward effect, not an evaluator instability. The two evaluators are stable where we can check them: they agree on human dialogue (§4.2, and Table 3, 0.51 vs. 0.57), and they continue to agree on generated dialogue when the reward is NLI-based — CALM scores 0.85-0.96 (SR-NLI) and 0.89-0.95 (SR-LLM). They only diverge when the reward is the LLM critic (GDP-Zero, DPDP: SR-LLM ~0.98, SR-NLI <0.1). So we observe the same two evaluators that agree on human dialogue keep agreeing on generated dialogue, unless a method optimizes the LLM critic specifically. We argue the divergence is produced by that optimization, not by the evaluation framework being unstable across the two metrics.

We also agree there is evidence the response distribution changes when the LLM agents take over, toward closure and agreeableness. We claim the divergence in metrics indicates this change is more pronounced when the agent is optimizing against an LLM critic. (We would also mention our observation in §4.2 that SR-NLI requires sustained entailment signal, whereas the LLM critic accepts very quick resolution, which DPDP’s authors also flagged as a failure mode.)

We again thank the reviewer for the response and constructive discussion. We will make the reward-vs-evaluator framing more explicit in §4.5, as it directly addresses the interpretability concern you raise.
