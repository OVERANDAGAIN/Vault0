## Title
Strong Empirical Evidence, but Limited Interpretation and Practical Scope


# Summary
This paper studies whether the structural dynamics of an ongoing human–LLM conversation, without access to lexical content, can provide useful signals about what the user will do next. The authors formulate a four-class next-turn event prediction task covering Continuation, Repair, Abandonment, and Gratitude, and model conversation structure using features derived from turn-taking patterns, message-length trajectories, user–assistant length ratios, and punctuation statistics.

The main empirical finding is that these content-free dynamics are effective for ranking imminent session endings, especially the Abandonment class, and outperform frozen content-based baselines. The paper further examines this finding through cross-platform evaluation on LMSYS-Chat-1M. It also compares the structural view with stronger content models and reports that the advantage on Abandonment persists. The main conclusion is that conversation structure provides an independent ranking signal for imminent session endings.


## Strengths

1. The empirical evaluation is comprehensive. Beyond the main comparison, the paper examines simple length and position effects, and evaluates whether the observed result persists on a disjoint WildChat slice and on LMSYS-Chat-1M.

2. The content-free formulation provides a clear and interpretable way to isolate structural signals in conversation. By restricting the input to turn structure, message-length trajectories, length ratios, and punctuation statistics, the paper allows the contribution of conversational structure to be studied separately from lexical content.

3. The advantage on Abandonment persists when stronger content models are considered, providing additional support for the main empirical finding.

## Weaknesses
1. The operational utility of the proposed signal is mainly limited to prioritization and auditing. Since Abandonment may correspond to qualitatively different outcomes, such as satisfied exits or users giving up, the ranking signal does not directly indicate whether intervention is desirable. The reported precision and recall under the top-\(k\) budgets also suggest that it is not yet suitable as a direct intervention signal.

2. The paper provides substantial evidence that structural dynamics carry predictive information, but the source of this signal is not characterized in much detail. In particular, the relative contributions of different structural feature families remain unclear. A more detailed analysis of which dynamics drive the Abandonment result would strengthen the interpretation of the main empirical finding.

## Justification of Recommendation

I find the paper technically sound and empirically well supported. The evaluation is extensive, and the main session-ending result appears robust across several controls and datasets. I remain slightly below the acceptance threshold because the practical scope is currently limited mainly to ranking and auditing, while the source of the structural advantage is not characterized in sufficient depth. These concerns limit the broader significance and interpretability of the contribution, although they do not undermine the main empirical finding.


## Specific Points of Feedback for Rebuttal

1. The main frozen content baselines use user-only text, whereas the dynamics representation also incorporates structural information from assistant turns. What motivated this choice, and how should the comparison between the two views be interpreted under this difference in input information?
2. In Table 2, the event labels are assigned sequentially, with the first matching rule determining the class. It would be helpful to explain why this particular ordering was chosen and whether the candidate rules overlap substantially before the priority is applied.
3. The paper gives clear principles for constructing the structural representation. Were the features specified independently of the main next-turn results, or did the feature set evolve during experimentation on the primary WildChat data?
